Tracking-by-Self Detection: SSL Animal Tracking

A self-supervised object detection and multiple object tracking (MOT) pipeline designed for farm livestock and constrained ecological environments, achieving mAP gains on consumer GPUs without expensive manual annotation.

Multi-target tracking showing stable identity bounding boxes across occluding pigs in real-time camera streams.

Research Motivation & Challenge

Animal detection and phenotyping are critical for animal welfare and behavioural science. However, manual frame-by-frame polygon annotation is extraordinarily labor-intensive. Most existing systems suffer when deployed to new farms or lighting conditions due to data scarcity.

To resolve this bottleneck, we engineered a self-supervised representation pipeline that pre-trains on massive volumes of unlabelled farm video. By modifying Barlow Twins cross-correlation objective and integrating an EfficientDet backbone, the detector achieves notable mAP gains across all confidence thresholds without inflating parameter counts or computational overhead.

Methodological Highlights

🧠

Barlow Twins SSL Pre-Training

Applies redundancy reduction across augmented image views, driving the cross-correlation matrix toward identity. Removes the need for negative pairs or memory banks, yielding resilient visual features.

🏷️

Automated Pseudo-Labelling

Constructed an automated temporal pseudo-labelling engine to extrapolate confident detections into continuous track trajectories across hours of unlabelled farm footage.

⚡

Consumer-GPU Real-Time Inference

Engineered the pipeline to achieve high FPS on non-industrial grade GPUs (e.g., standard consumer RTX cards), making on-farm online edge deployment viable.
